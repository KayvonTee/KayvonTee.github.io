

When people say college changes you, they are right. I look back to my first 2 years of college and also back to high school, and although these years of my life are still recent, I feel that I have little resemblance to myself from just a couple of years ago. The person I am now has grown from the teenager I once was and is now becoming a young adult. How I perceive the world, how I carry myself, my connections with family and peers, and my responsibilities have all noticeably changed from just a few years ago.

Interestingly while cognitively I have changed a lot, I have seen myself mostly stay the same physically. When photos of myself from 11th and 12th grade occasionally show up on my phone's photo memories, I would think that my appearance had not changed much at all since then. This is especially true compared to some of my friends whose appearances have changed significantly since just a few years ago. Up until a few months ago I would say that my appearance has not changed much; my face is still teenagerly, I weigh the same as I was in high school, and I even wear a lot of the same clothes from high school. It has only been recently in the past few months that I believe that my physical appearance has changed since a few years ago.   

While I have grown as an individual in many ways, there is one major realization within myself that I think many pieces of my identity can be attributed to and continues to influence my maturing into a young adult. 

Growing up as a kid, its pretty normal for everyone to question their ego as they become more conscious of themselves in their environments. I remember having these thoughts too as I would question myself and my confidence. As a kid, its easy to relate ego to how others perceive you or how good you are at something. I fell into this trap, as I'm sure many of my other peers also did. 

As a kid, I played soccer and tennis competitively. With tennis becoming my main sport starting in middle school and onwards; my confidence was tightly interconnected with how good I was at tennis and how good others thought I was. I think the individuality of tennis as a sport also added to this association of my confidence and tennis. As you can imagine, this association caused many ups and downs in my own confidence. While my level of tennis was good, I would have more confidence in myself; I was steady and balanced during these times. When I played in a tournament and lost big, or to someone I thought I should beat, my confidence would tank, and stay like this for a while. One time, I remember in 8th grade I had a tournament right before the end of school year dance. I lost pretty bad in my match, something like 1-6 2-6. My confidence was so hurt that I decided to not go to the school dance that day. My parents insisted on me going, but I had convinced myself that I shouldn't go -- that my ego had been burned and now I did not want to socialize with my friends that day. I would have to explain that I lost badly, and that would change their perception of how good I was at tennis. Tennis had become my overarching identity. I had many more instances of dramatic drops in my confidence, and after these events, it would take time for me to become steady again. 

Now as a 21 year old, I am thankful to be able to reflect and recognize how flawed my former view of self-confidence was. Associating tennis directly to my self-confidence was very short-sighted, and for that matter, any similar associations would yield the same result. This way of living is suffering, constantly in fear of one's ego getting hurt. I have found inner peace no longer falling for these superficial thoughts and glad to say I do not live like this. 

Today, my self-confidence is not rooted in how others perceive me, nor how good I am at something. Within myself, I know that I conduct my life with pure intentions, and do not define myself by constraints. Knowing these truths about myself is freeing. There are no worries of damaging my ego, and I never have to question who I am. I do not care what others perceive me as, for, I know who I am, and if they are unable to see me authentically, its a reflection of themselves, not me. 


These principles for my confidence and self-perception have bled into other parts of my identity. Engaging in the world without worry is calming, and has affected how I carry myself, how I interact with the people around me, and my outlook on the world. Living without worry and constraints opens so many opportunities and allows for my authentic self, and I look forwards to more personal development in this direction.
